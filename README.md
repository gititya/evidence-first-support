# Experiments in AI and customer support

I am a support professional who builds prototypes to test my assumptions about AI in support. I keep the negative results and explain where the evidence stops.

The question behind this work is practical: what should good support preserve when more of it is handled or assisted by AI?

## Helping the next person continue

**Handoff Quality Gate** makes parts of a useful transfer explicit: what happened, what support knows, what remains uncertain and what the next person should do. Urgent exceptions need accountability; an unresolved cause does not automatically mean a bad handoff.

## Checking what support did

**Quality Agency** tests small AI reviewers against support requirements, including policy use, promises and handoffs. Its mistakes matter as much as its overall score: which bad replies did the reviewer approve?

**Support Evals** checks recorded support journeys. It asks whether decisions had evidence when they were made, and whether claimed actions and outcomes have supporting records. The final assessment of the connected Voice and Copilot experiences remains open.

## Understanding the operational effect

**Capacity Planning** explores what happens when AI takes some incoming work. Contacts removed, human workload and total cost are different numbers. The model tests assumptions; it does not predict a universal staffing outcome.

**Signal** turns public complaint patterns into possible product investigations. Complaints show what people report, not proof of a cause. A targeted check confirmed that the revised brief leaves investigation completion unknown when the complaints do not establish it. That is a limited result, not proof of general reliability.

## Keeping the experiments that changed my mind

**Intent Classifier** achieved strong results on template-like examples and struggled with ordinary customer wording in a small reused set. I kept the gap as the lesson rather than starting another training round.

**Early Prediction** tested whether a call's opening revealed its specific cause. It usually identified the broad problem, but rarely the cause in these fictional cases. That led to a different question: how can AI help a rep investigate as evidence arrives?

## Work still in progress

**Copilot Lab** explores that investigation question for a human support rep. It assists the rep privately; it does not speak to the customer. Its bounded scenario-based experiment now covers resolution and engineering escalation, with a positive owner walkthrough of changing advice. Broader rep benefit remains unmeasured.

**Voice Support** aims to investigate, guide a customer inside an app, check whether it helped and carry the case to a person when needed. The foundation has local checks, but the complete live-app experience and final demonstration remain unfinished.

These are prototypes and experiments intended to make my support reasoning inspectable. They are not claims of production performance or proof that every support operation should work this way.
